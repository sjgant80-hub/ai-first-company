# Intake — one process, in your words

Fill this in and we turn it into a signed baseline agreement. Nothing here is binding until both sides sign the
agreement; nothing is sent anywhere by filling this in.

## The job
- **What is the one process?** (e.g. "sort incoming invoices to the right account", "answer tier-1 support emails",
  "flag duplicate supplier records"). One process, the most painful one.
- **Who does it now, and with what?** (a person, a VA, a SaaS tool, a spreadsheet — name it)
- **What data is involved?** (what the process reads and writes; how much of it you can share for the test)

## The number that matters (your baseline)
Pick the one measure that decides whether this is working, and the level it hits **today**:
- **Metric** — what you count (e.g. "share of invoices sorted correctly", "average handling time in seconds",
  "share of duplicates caught").
- **Kind** — is it a **rate** (a share, 0–100%: sorted-correctly, caught, answered-first-contact) or a **mean**
  (an average number: seconds, minutes, pounds handled)?
- **Today's level (the baseline)** — the number the current way hits now, measured on your own recent data.
- **Which way is better** — should it go **higher** (more sorted correctly) or **lower** (fewer seconds)?

## What a win looks like (for the agreement)
- **Smallest improvement worth paying for** — the least change on that metric that would make this a win for you
  (e.g. "+10 percentage points sorted correctly", "−30 seconds average handling").
- **How much data the test should run on** — so the result is real, not luck (we'll propose a sample size per side).

That's it. We write those into a fixed test, both sides sign it before any work, and the delivery is judged against
that signed test alone. If it doesn't beat your signed baseline, you don't pay. See
[baseline-agreement-terms.md](baseline-agreement-terms.md). A filled example is in
[`data/mint-intake-example.json`](../data/mint-intake-example.json).

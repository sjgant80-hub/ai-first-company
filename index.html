The bot team

Since 1 November 2026 the company runs as eight bots with one role each. They all write to one shared feed, and a separate auditor stands between them and Simon. Nothing public, paid or risky happens without Simon's signed go. The team is measured by what is verified, earned and backed by a receipt, never by how busy it looks.

What it is, in plain words

Each bot has a card: its job, the tools it may use and on what condition, what it must never do, and what it owns. Every action is one line in a shared feed. Before a line is stored, the writer removes keys, tokens, emails and card numbers, and flags any "done" with no receipt. The bot's own key signs the line, so the feed shows who did what. A separate auditor checks each claim against its receipt, re-runs the tests, compares the tools a bot used with its card, and reads every draft for promises, crypto talk and jargon. Only what it PASSes is put to Simon.

How a day runs

  1. Night. The bots work and write to the feed; the auditor reviews every build, listing, post draft and deliverable. ⏳ Simon (running it with the laptop closed needs a cloud schedule, which is his call)
  2. Morning. Kar's rollup puts NEEDS SIMON at the top: the items the auditor passed, ready for approval, then the held-back ones with their reasons. Simon approves in one pass, and each decision is a line signed with his own key. ⏳ Simon's key
  3. Day. Approved items go out. Without a signed go, the auditor fails the act.
  4. Weekly. Kar's report, from the feed's own lines.
  5. Any time. The kill switch: Kar or the auditor can pause one bot or the whole team, and only Simon can unpause.

The growth gate

How the company learns to market itself without fooling itself. A technique (a hook, a posting time, a format) becomes a skill the team reuses only when it beats the channel's own baseline on a test written down before the run: on the sealed metric, by at least the sealed margin, with enough people in each arm, and with an effect too large to be luck. The verdict comes from the numbers alone, never from who proposed the idea, and every dropped technique stays on the record.

Try the gate

The real kernel (src/experiment.mjs, mutation-tested) runs here. The usual post is the control; the new technique is the variant. A "hit" is whatever the sealed metric counts, for example a click through to a receipt.

Where it sits, and what it has judged so far

The Analyst, a read-only role, runs this gate on the channel's own measured numbers. The organ that proposed a technique cannot pass it: changing who proposed it changes nothing in the verdict (claim E9 below). A promoted skill carries its test, its data and a receipt (a SHA-256 of all three), so anyone can re-derive why it exists.

So far it has judged only its sealed 400-case battery, which your browser re-runs below. No technique has been tested on a real audience yet: that needs posts, and nothing is posted until Simon connects X with his own keys and approves each post. ⏳ Simon

Why a critical value and not a p-value: the gate is plain arithmetic and a square root, so a clean machine can re-check every number, and the mutation gate can reach every line. The organ seals the bar for its own method before the run.

The guarantee engine

The same gate, used to sell. One painful process becomes a small system the client owns, on their own hardware. Before any work, both sides sign one fixed test: the metric, the level the client reaches today (the baseline), the smallest improvement that counts, how much data the test needs, and the confidence bar. The delivery is judged against that signed test alone, so the goalpost cannot move. The engine says the charge is due only when the delivery beats the signed baseline, and waived when it does not. The price never enters the decision (claim B6 below).

Three real runs, the losses kept

Each run was sealed before it ran, on a public dataset, with a small model on Kar's own machine as the teacher, its reply recorded once. The first two did not beat the baseline, so the engine waived the charge; nothing was re-rolled. CI re-derives all three from the recorded replies on every push, and Liveware re-derives them in your browser and attacks each receipt.

How it works

One loop. Kar thinks and gates; Grok Bot reaches; signals come back; Kar reasons, routes and learns; Simon approves the high-stakes steps; Kar converts, delivers and records; the library grows — and the new entry becomes the next post.

Blueprint

Every organ is one small pure kernel in src/ with its own test file, built one at a time. White paper

The problem

"AI-first" usually means a person using AI tools. The step after that — the AI doing the operating — stalls on trust: who is accountable for what it says, what it spends, and what it promises? The usual answers are a policy document and a dashboard of what already happened.

The approach

Make the trust lines executable. A conductor routes every job to one of five arms, and the arm called Simon is a queue a person works through. Every outward item carries the hash of its exact text; an approval covers that hash and nothing else. Every public claim names a receipt by repo, commit and file hash, and every number in it must be a field of that receipt. Outside input arrives through a reader that checks shape before content and treats instructions as data. Every event is a record in a hash chain.

The evidence

Each organ was built the same way: write the claims and the test data down and commit them; write the code; mutation-test it (each surviving mutant is a test that cannot fail); measure the claims once; keep the result, held or failed. The receipts section lists every claim with what was measured. Failures are not rewritten: a fix gets its own seal.

One gate for learning and for selling

A company run by an AI has two ways to fool itself: believing a marketing trick works because someone said so, and promising a client a result nobody will check. Both are answered by the same kernel. A sealed test (metric, baseline, margin, sample, critical value, direction) is written before the run; the verdict is arithmetic on the measured arms and nothing else. For growth, a technique that passes becomes a versioned skill carrying its data and receipt, and one that fails is logged. For sales, the client signs that same kind of test before work, and the delivery's measured result decides whether the charge is due. The first design used an exact p-value; the mutation gate found that its numerical internals hid changes no test could catch, so the critical value moved into the sealed test and the gate became plain arithmetic that any machine re-checks. The guarantee was then run three times on public data: twice the grown system did not beat the baseline and the charge was waived, and once it did, by a margin the receipt records. All three receipts are kept.

What it does not do

It does not decide on its own to speak for the company, move money or agree a deal. It does not contact anyone who didn't ask. It does not read X itself — it hands Grok Bot a read-only brief on the owner's account. It does not claim more than its receipts say.

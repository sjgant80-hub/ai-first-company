For your business

The same shape works for any company that wants the AI to do the operating and a person to keep the say.

What it does for you

The work between "someone asked about us" and "it's delivered and recorded" runs on its own: watching, drafting, qualifying, preparing offers, building the deliverable, keeping the books of what happened. Your people approve the moments that matter — what goes out in your name, money, deals — in one queue, with the exact words in front of them.

How you roll it out

  1. Start with one channel and one offer, in dry run: nothing leaves, everything is shown.
  2. Seal what "working" means before you measure it (the receipts above are the template).
  3. Connect your own channel keys. Approve item by item for the first weeks.
  4. Add the next offer only when the first one has a paying customer.

Who decides what

The AI decides routing, drafting, qualifying and delivery. A person decides anything outward, any money, any deal, anything risky or new, and the direction (what to watch, which offer to lead with, pause everything). Those lines are code — the conductor's lanes — not a policy document.

What it will not do

Cold outreach, automated replies to people who didn't ask, scraping a website, acting on an instruction found inside someone's post, or posting a number that isn't in a receipt. Each of those is refused in code, and the refusals are tested.

The case

Chief executive

A company that keeps working between your decisions, and a single queue where your decisions are asked for. Every public claim is one you can stand behind, because it is pinned to evidence anyone can fetch.

Finance

Money never moves without a person: money verbs and amounts route to approval, and the ledger has no switch that turns on value. The cost case below is computed from the UK median wage (ONS), not from a sales estimate.

Operations

Every step is one record in a hash chain: who did it (Kar, Grok Bot, Simon), what, and when. One changed byte breaks the chain at that record, so the history cannot be quietly edited.

Technology and security

Small pure kernels, each mutation-tested (a test that can't fail is caught). Outside input is checked before it is read; instructions inside it are treated as data. Channel keys stay in the owner's environment — the code never prints or stores them, and that is measured.

Marketing and sales

The voice posts what has been proven, answers people who ask, and never cold-pitches. That is also exactly where X draws its own automation line, so the account is safe by construction.

Data protection

Signals from X are public posts; email addresses and phone numbers are redacted on the way in. Nothing private goes into a public token or a public page — the passport's leak gate and this repo's checks refuse it.

Questions a buyer asks first

  • Isn't one approver a bottleneck? By design, one named person approves what leaves the company. When they are away, nothing outward goes — items wait in the queue, and everything inside the company keeps running. At one cycle a working day that is about thirty approvals a week, each one the exact words in front of them.
  • Who else runs this? Nobody yet. This is the first deployment, run on the supplier's own company: the dashboard is a measured rehearsal, not a customer. The evidence is the receipts — sealed before the code, re-measured by CI on every change. A pilot starts in dry run, so you see everything before anything leaves.
  • What is a "dry run", exactly? Everything happens except the one network call to X: the post or reply is drafted, gated, asked for, approved and written to the ledger marked as a rehearsal. Nothing is sent. The connector only calls X when the owner's keys are present and the live switch is on and the item carries the owner's approval of its exact words.
  • What does Grok Bot do, exactly? One routine, on the owner's own Grok Bot, every two hours: read public posts on X that mention the company or ask about its topics (Grok Bot's public X access is read-only), and append one line per post to one file in one repo. It never posts, replies, likes, follows or messages anyone, and it is told that a post is data even when it claims to come from the owner. Read the brief it is given. Kar checks every line before reading it.
  • Where are the keys? In the owner's own environment. The code reads them only to sign the request to X, never writes them to a file, a log or the screen — measured with fake keys planted through every scenario: 0 occurrences anywhere it wrote or printed (kar-voice's claim V6).
  • What data does it keep, and does an AI judge people? It keeps public posts that mention the company — handle and text — with email addresses and phone numbers redacted on the way in. Which offer fits is chosen from the person's own words by plain code, not by a model's opinion of them; models only draft wording, and every draft is checked and approved before it goes.
  • Where has it got things wrong? On the page, not hidden: the receipts above show each claim that failed and why — the router letting 39 of 57 outward-sounding jobs past on fresh wording, the reader flagging 7 of 10 trick posts (the other 3 were ordinary questions), a sealed brief that named a private repository. Each failure stays in the record; a fix gets its own seal.
  • How often is the owner involved? In the measured cycle: six asks — the post, the reply, the offer, the close, the hand-over, and the next post — and every other step ran without him (the dashboard above reads the exact count from the cycle's receipt). At one cycle a working day that is about thirty approvals a week, each a yes to words already on screen.
  • Is "no cold outreach" strict, or are there exceptions? Strict, with no switch: there is no code that contacts someone who has not named the company in their own post. A post Grok Bot says is for us, but which does not name us, is a market signal only — that is measured (claims R3 and G2).
  • How does "fail closed" work, and how often does it fail? A job runs on its own only if its first verb is a known piece of internal work (draft, review, compile, measure…); any other verb waits for the owner. Every outward, money or deal word sends a job to the owner wherever it appears. On the sets it was built against it routes every case right; on fresh wording, the small model used to write test jobs mislabelled too many of its own lines for the test to say — both results are in the receipts.
  • When does the dry run end? When the owner decides: he adds his own X keys and turns the live switch on. Until both, nothing changes and nothing leaves.
  • Can we change what it says? Yes. Posts, replies and offers are plain templates in the code, written from your receipts and your offer pages; you edit them. Every draft, edited or not, still passes the claim gate and still needs the owner's approval of its exact words.
  • Can I set my own numbers? Yes: the hours, your own wage and your seat count above all recompute in the page, with the same code the receipts were measured with.

Risks, answered straight

  • The router can misjudge wording. Measured: on wording written by a small model after the code was frozen, the first version let 39 of 57 outward jobs past; the fail-closed fix held every seen set but its fresh test failed too, partly on the test model's own mislabelled lines. That is why the router is the fence and the connector is the wall: nothing leaves without an approval of the exact item, whatever the router decided.
  • A receipt shows a number, not the words around it. The claim gate proves every number is the receipt's number at a named field; whether the sentence describes it fairly is what the human approval of the exact text is for.
  • Grok Bot is xAI's product on the owner's account. It is given a read-only job and a single file to write; Kar re-checks everything it hands back and decides "summoned" itself.
  • Approvals are recorded by Kar from the owner's words in chat. They are quoted and bound to the text's hash; they are not a signature by the owner.

White paper

The problem

"AI-first" usually means a person using AI tools. The step after that — the AI doing the operating — stalls on trust: who is accountable for what it says, what it spends, and what it promises? The usual answers are a policy document and a dashboard of what already happened.

The approach

Make the trust lines executable. A conductor routes every job to one of five arms, and the arm called Simon is a queue a person works through. Every outward item carries the hash of its exact text; an approval covers that hash and nothing else. Every public claim names a receipt by repo, commit and file hash, and every number in it must be a field of that receipt. Outside input arrives through a reader that checks shape before content and treats instructions as data. Every event is a record in a hash chain.

The evidence

Each organ was built the same way: write the claims and the test data down and commit them; write the code; mutation-test it (each surviving mutant is a test that cannot fail); measure the claims once; keep the result, held or failed. The receipts section lists every claim with what was measured. Failures are not rewritten: a fix gets its own seal.

What it does not do

It does not decide on its own to speak for the company, move money or agree a deal. It does not contact anyone who didn't ask. It does not read X itself — it hands Grok Bot a read-only brief on the owner's account. It does not claim more than its receipts say.
